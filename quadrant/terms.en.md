## 1. Features and scope

Quadorder manages tasks locally in four quadrants, with dates, reminders, repeat rules, and the last 30 days of completion history. A repeating task generates its next occurrence when you complete the current occurrence; it is not a calendar-based automatic generation service. Basic use requires no app account. The app currently provides no cloud sync of its own across devices.

Current free capacity is 4 active tasks per quadrant. A one-time unlock increases this to 8 per quadrant. Appearance, language, and reminders are not separately charged based on unlock status. Available features, supported platforms, and purchase contents depend on the installed version and purchase confirmation screen. ## 3. Tasks, reminders, and backups

You retain rights to your task content and should only enter, import, or share content you are entitled to use.

Tasks and completion history are primarily stored on your device. Completion history is limited to the last 30 days; the app removes older records on launch or return to the foreground. Export backups regularly if you need a longer record and keep those files safe.

Restoring a backup replaces current tasks and completion history. It does not merge datasets or restore language, appearance, system permissions, or purchase entitlements. The replacement cannot be undone in the app, and history outside the retention window is removed during restoration. Export current data and check the file before restoring. File validation cannot determine whether a backup reflects your intended data.

Permissions, notification settings, Focus modes, device conditions, and system services may affect reminder delivery. Local notifications whose scheduled time has passed are not replayed. Quadorder is a general task manager and should not be your sole reminder for personal safety, emergencies, or other critical matters. These explanations do not exclude product liability required by law.

Restoring an earlier repeating task may be refused if later occurrences have been completed or edited, to protect subsequent work. Resolve the most recent linked occurrence before retrying an earlier restoration.
